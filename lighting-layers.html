Five Layers of Architectural Lighting

A practical framework for combining daylight, ambient, task, accent and decorative light.

Interior design becomes more useful when aesthetic choices are tested against time, behavior and maintenance—not only against the opening-day photograph.

Start with daylight

Before adding fixtures, map where daylight arrives, where glare occurs and which tasks happen at different times of day.

Build layers, not brightness

Ambient light establishes a base level. Task light supports specific work. Accent light creates hierarchy. Decorative light adds character. No single layer should do every job.

Control is part of design

Dimming and simple scene control can change the experience of a room more than adding additional fixtures.
