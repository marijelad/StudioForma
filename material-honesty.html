Material Honesty: Designing for Patina

Why surfaces that change with use often create richer interiors than finishes designed to remain untouched.

Interior design becomes more useful when aesthetic choices are tested against time, behavior and maintenance—not only against the opening-day photograph.

Why patina matters

Materials change whether designers plan for it or not. Timber warms, stone gains marks, metal oxidizes and fabrics soften. Treating those changes as part of the design creates interiors that become more convincing over time.

Choose surfaces for the real routine

A finish should be judged against hands, water, sunlight, cleaning, shoes, pets and maintenance—not only against a sample under studio lighting.

Design for repair

Replaceable components, accessible fixings and finishes that can be refinished extend the useful life of interiors and reduce the fear of everyday wear.
